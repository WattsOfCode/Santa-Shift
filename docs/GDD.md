# Santa Shift Game Design Document

Game Off 2026, production reference.
**Version 0.2, November 7, 2026.** Version 0.1 (November 2) was the original proposal. This version brings it in line
with the game as built. Every change from 0.1 has a dated entry in [decisions.md](decisions.md) with its reason; the
sections below state the current rule only, so nothing here contradicts the build. Where a rule is proposed but not
built yet it is marked **(proposed)**.

---

## 1 Purpose and decision status

Santa Shift is a solo, third-person 3D workshop game about an elf finishing Christmas orders while Santa makes
increasingly unreasonable production demands. A run is a series of five-minute shifts (rounds); each one met moves the
elf to a harder one. The goal is readable actions, satisfying toy deliveries and short workplace comedy, made with free,
code-first tools and a small set of original assets.

This document is the reference for design, implementation, content, testing and release. Mechanics and numbers are
playtest hypotheses, not proven values.

**Confirmed constraints:** the Santa Shift concept; programming rather than a visual editor; free tools; solo scope;
Game Off submission.

**Current toolchain (decided 2026-11-06):** C# on .NET 10 with Raylib-cs (no engine), Blender for models (generated by
Python scripts), Windows desktop build. Keyboard and mouse, or a controller.

**Tuning values** (speeds, deadlines, quotas, timings, score bands) live in JSON files under `Content/data/`, never as
constants scattered through code.

**Change rule:** a feature is added only if it replaces comparable work or fixes a demonstrated problem. Consequential
changes get a decision-log entry (Section 28), and this document is updated in the same change.

## 3 Design pillars and theme

- **Readable pressure.** Players always know what they are carrying, what each order needs, and why a delivery
  succeeds or fails. Pressure comes from decisions and travel, not hidden rules.
- **Small actions feel good.** Collecting a kit, hammering parts on, tying a ribbon and sending a toy down the chute each
  get immediate animation and sound. Polish these before adding content.
- **Santa is the problem.** His changing demands generate the comedy. No jokes at the player's expense for accessibility
  settings, slow learning or honest mistakes.
- **Fair changes.** New policies apply only to orders created under them. Existing orders keep their requirements; a toy
  the player has started is never silently invalidated. Station-level changes (proposed, Section 10.3) always get a
  5-second warning and never touch the carried item.

**Theme: SHIFT.** Three connected readings: the elf works a scheduled *shift*; Santa *shifts* production rules during
it; and each round *shifts* the work itself (the bench parts change order, then change keys). Shown through the shift
clock, policy banners, requirement badges, the round counter and the review. Keyboard Shift is not a control.

---

## 4 Player fantasy and emotional arc

The player is a capable elf dealing with absurd management. The cabin is cosy and easy to read. Orders arrive, Santa
announces requirements, and the player starts making deliberate trade-offs. The last seconds of each shift are a
delivery rush; between rounds the player picks a perk and feels stronger, then the next round pushes back. The review
credits the player's real results even when Santa claims them.

Arc: curiosity → first delivery (guided) → confidence → comic disruption → controlled scramble → relief, a perk, and
"one more round".

No cutscenes or branching conversations.

## 6 Core loop and a sample order

Read an order → take its kit → bench: hammer the parts on → add a ribbon in the right colour if required → ship →
score and feedback → choose the next order.

Variants: a **naughty** child's order wants a lump of coal (shovel it at the fireplace, ship it). A **botched** toy goes
to the recycling barrel. An **empty kit bin** (the shift's kits were wasted, or the lesson's planned shortfall) needs a
supply crate from the sled. With the quota met, the elf can **punch out** at the clock by the door and end the shift
early (Section 12).

Example: a card shows a train, a gold ribbon badge and 65 seconds. The elf takes a train kit and hammers on the wheels,
boiler and cab on the keys shown. At shipping, an early attempt explains "Needs a gold ribbon" without consuming the toy.
At the ribbon station the camera leans in over the spools; the player clicks gold (or presses its key), holds still for a
second, returns and ships.

The interesting decision is which order to work on and how to route the room. Walking stays short so time goes into
choices.

## 7 Controls and interaction rules

| Action | Keyboard / mouse | Controller | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Look | Mouse; Z / C turn | Right stick; LT / RT turn | Camera orbits behind the elf; swings round on its own after 2 s facing one way |
| Move | WASD / arrows | Left stick / D-pad | Relative to the camera; same speed in all directions |
| Interact | E / Space | A | One contextual action on the highlighted fixture |
| Bench parts / ribbon colours | 1 / 2 / 3 (or click a spool) | X / Y / RB | Hammer the shown part; pick a spool |
| Pause / back | Esc | Start / B | Pauses the simulation; opens the pause menu or closes the current menu |
| Menus | Arrows, Enter, Esc | D-pad, A, B | Visible focus; all menus usable without a mouse |

Everything except pause and menu keys can be rebound (Settings > Controls). Prompts always show the current binding of
the device used last.

Discrete actions use pressed-edge input; holding Interact never repeats. A press nobody reads within 0.25 s is
dropped, so a title-screen press can't fire in the workshop.

Only one fixture is selected at a time: the nearest within 54 logical pixels of its interaction point, ties broken by
fixture ID. It gets an outline and a named prompt ("E Build train", not "Use").

The elf carries one item, held up on its right hand beside its head and named in the HUD slot. No dropping, jumping or
sprinting. A full hand at a bin gives "Hands full".

Recycling: the first press opens "Recycle carried item?"; Interact again confirms, Esc or moving cancels. Confirming
clears the item with no score loss. Empty-handed: "Nothing to recycle".

Punch clock: the same two-press confirmation. The prompt says whether the quota is met ("Punch out early (quota met)")
or not ("Punch out early (4 of 9 delivered)"); short of quota, the question warns that punching out ends the run. Esc,
moving or pausing cancels. Hands may be full; the item is simply left behind.

## 8 Items and crafting

| Toy ID | Look | Parts (natural order) |
|---|---|---|
| train | Red locomotive | wheels, boiler, cab |
| bear | Brown bear | body, head, arms |
| robot | Teal robot | legs, torso, head |

**Item** = toy ID, stage, ribbon flag and ribbon colour. Stages: `kit`, `assembled`, `botched`, `coal`, `supply` (a
supply crate).

**Kit bins (Santa's sack).** When a shift starts, Santa decides who writes in that shift (one letter per scheduled
order, Section 9) and fills the bins **once**: one kit for every toy a nice child will ask for. Nothing tops them up during
the shift; the only way to get more kits is a supply crate from the sled. A careful shift therefore never needs the sled,
and every botched or scrapped kit leaves that bin one short. Each shift (round) gets a fresh sack; leftover kits are not
carried over. The label shows how many are left and turns red when the bin is empty and a live order needs that toy.
Slow shifts that skip spawn attempts end with kits left over, never short. Without a sled in `stations.json`, bins never
run out.

**Toy pictures.** Every kit crate (on four sides) and every bin's sign board shows its toy's picture: the order-card
silhouette on a white card framed in the toy's colour. They are painted when the game starts from the same icons the
cards use, so a toy added to `toys.json` gets its picture without an image file. Crates are also tinted the toy's colour,
so shape and colour both say which kit it is.

**Assembly bench (minigame).** Interact with a kit to start. The camera moves in over the elf's shoulder and the three
parts are shown in order, each on a key. Hammer them on in that order before the time limit runs out.
- Time limit: 6.0 s in round 1, 0.5 s less each round, never below 2.5 s.
- From round 2 the part order is random each build; from round 4 the parts also move to different keys.
- Relaxed mode plays one round behind with 1.5× the time.
- A wrong part or running out of time **botches** the toy (it must be recycled; Christmas Spirit −8).
- Walking away cancels with the kit intact. Pause stops the timer.

**Ribbon station.** Accepts an assembled toy with no ribbon. The camera leans in over the spools (red, gold, green;
`tuning.json > ribbonColors`). Pick one by clicking it or pressing its key, then hold still 1.0 s. Moving cancels with the
toy intact. Ribbons are unlimited.

**Coal scuttle.** Empty-handed, hold still 0.8 s to shovel a lump of coal. Coal ships like a toy to a naughty child's
order.

**Supply sled.** Empty-handed, take a supply crate; carry it to a kit bin to add 2 kits (`resupplyKits`).

**Shipping.** Accepts an assembled toy (or coal) matching a live order: same toy, the required ribbon colour if any (an
extra ribbon is fine). If several match: earliest deadline, then oldest, then smallest ID. No manual order choice.

A rejected shipment never consumes the item, changes the score or resets a deadline, and explains why: build it first,
add a ribbon, wrong colour, no matching order. An expired order can't be fulfilled; the toy may still suit another.

Transitions: empty → kit → assembled (or botched) → ribboned if wanted → empty after delivery or recycling. Empty →
coal → empty. Empty → supply crate → empty at a bin. Invalid transitions are rejected; nothing duplicates an item.

## 9 Orders and scheduling

Each card shows the child's name, toy icon and name, seconds left, a ribbon badge in the required colour, a RUSH badge,
or a coal badge. All live cards are visible, sorted by earliest deadline.

**Schedule** (seconds after the shift starts): 0, 18, 36 … 252 (15 attempts). An attempt creates one order only if fewer
than three are live; otherwise it is skipped (no backlog, not counted as missed).

**Letters and the shift plan.** At the start of each run, Santa's naughty and nice list marks 3 children naughty; their
orders want coal. At the start of each shift the order plan is fixed: the n-th order created takes the toy sequence (train,
bear, robot) and the next letter for that toy from `letters.json`, and is coal if that child is naughty. Orders are created
from the plan in order (a skipped attempt does not skip a plan entry). Ribbon and rush are still decided when each order is
created, from the events active at that moment. The plan is what stocks the kit bins (Section 8).

**Guided lesson.** In round 1, until the player has cleared a round once, the first six orders are arranged to show
every job: plain toys, coal for a naughty child as the 3rd, a ribbon as the 5th, and **a resupply on the 6th**. They are
real orders and count towards the quota (this replaces 0.1's separate untimed practice order).
- The lesson round's sack is one kit short. The missing kit is for the 6th order's toy (the robot with the shipped data).
- After the lesson, that toy is left out of the round's toy sequence, so the shortfall is exactly one kit.
- Its bin therefore runs dry just as the 6th order arrives. The guide sends the elf to the sled, then to that bin, then
  back to work.
- One crate holds 2 kits, so one is left over. Every other bin holds everything the round needs.
- `tuning.json > tutorial.shortKit` turns this off.

**Deadlines:** 55 s; ribbon orders 65 s; rush 45 s. Relaxed mode ×1.5. Each round multiplies deadlines by 0.95 (never
below 0.7). Perks can lengthen them.

**Expiry** removes the card, counts a miss, resets the streak, costs Christmas Spirit (−20) and shows "Order expired". It
never touches the carried item. At shift end, live orders close as unfinished (counted separately, no penalty). Every
created order reaches exactly one of delivered, missed or unfinished.

## 10 Santa events and changing rules

Santa is a portrait and announcement strip in the HUD; he never stands on the floor.

### 10.1 Built schedule (every round)

| Warning | Active | Event | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 55 s | 60 to 90 s | Presentation initiative | New orders need ribbons |
| 115 s | 120 s until the next created order | Executive priority | The next created order is RUSH |
| 175 s | 180 to 210 s | Festive compliance | New orders need ribbons |
| 235 s | 240 s until the next created order | Last minute promise | The next created order is RUSH |

Ribbon windows include their start and exclude their end. Requirements are copied onto an order when it is created and
never changed. A rush token survives skipped attempts and is used only when an order is created; it does not carry past
shift end. Rush deliveries earn +25.

The warning states the exact effect 5 s ahead; the active banner is compact, with a captioned chime. Flavour lines fade
after 5 s; the requirement stays on the card.

### 10.2 Rules for any rule change

1. **Announce first.** A 5-second warning naming the exact effect, then a banner while it lasts.
2. **New orders only**, or a fixture-level change with a warning. Never change a live order or the carried item.
3. **Visible on the card or the fixture**: a badge, a label, a lit or dark station. Never audio-only, never colour-only.
4. **No arbitrary penalties.** Santa makes work harder or stranger, not unfair.
5. **One active rule change at a time**, apart from a pending rush token.
6. **Data-driven:** each is an event type in `events.json`, tested in `EventTests`.

### 10.3 Proposed rule changes (SHIFT) **(proposed)**

Today every round has the same timetable, so the "boss shifts the rules" pitch is weaker than it could be. Proposal:
keep the four built events in round 1 (they match the lesson), then from round 2 Santa draws 4 events per round from a
pool, seeded per run, with harder ones unlocking in later rounds. Each round's draw is shown on the round-start card
("Today's memo: …") so players can plan.

| # | Event (Santa's memo) | Effect | Fairness / readability | From round | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Brand refresh** | For 30 s, new ribbon orders all want one colour (e.g. gold) | Badge on the card; a banner in that colour | 2 | Small: colour override on ribbon windows |
| 2 | **Toy craze** | For 30 s, every new order is the same toy | Bin for that toy glows; tests kit stock and the sled | 2 | Small: override the toy sequence |
| 3 | **Coal audit** | The next 2 new orders are coal (any child) | Coal badge on the card; scuttle highlighted | 2 | Small: a coal token like the rush token |
| 4 | **VIP letter** | The next new order is VIP: 40 s deadline, double points | Gold VIP badge on the card | 3 | Small: new order flag and score rule |
| 5 | **Retooled bench** | For 30 s, the bench parts move to different keys (as round 4+ does) | Banner; the bench panel already shows each key | 2 (before round 4) | Small: existing key-shuffle switch |
| 6 | **Sled delayed** | For 30 s, the supply sled is closed | Sled dark with a "BACK AT 2:10" sign; carried crates still work | 3 | Small: station-disabled flag |
| 7 | **Chute relocated** | For 40 s, shipping moves to a second chute across the room | Old chute shows "MOVED →" with a guide arrow; held toys are unaffected | 4 | Medium: second shipping fixture and model |
| 8 | **Cocoa break** | Clock and deadlines freeze for 10 s; the elf can still work | Relief beat; the banner counts down | 3 | Small: freeze order timers |
| 9 | **Overtime** | The shift runs 30 s longer and ends with a rush order | Clock shows "+0:30" | 4 | Medium: extendable shift end and one extra spawn |
| 10 | **Quality inspection** | For 30 s, deliveries with a full time bonus earn +25 | Stopwatch badge on the cards | 3 | Small: score rule in the window |

**Recommended first five:** 1 Brand refresh, 2 Toy craze, 5 Retooled bench, 6 Sled delayed and 8 Cocoa break. They
reuse existing systems, cover every station, and include a relief beat. 7 Chute relocated is the most literal "shift"
and the best trailer moment, but it needs a new fixture.

If tuning shows interruptions are too hard: lengthen deadlines, draw 3 events instead of 4, or drop the second rush,
before changing anything else.

## 11 Timing and fairness

One simulation clock (60 Hz fixed step) drives shift time, deadlines, crafting and events. Rendering runs at any rate;
catch-up is capped after a stall. Pause and losing window focus stop all of it. Resume clears held input and runs a
one-second READY countdown with the simulation still stopped.

Within each step: detect shift end first (then finalise; no further actions), then event starts and ends, new orders,
movement and crafting, shipments, then expiry. A delivery on an order's exact deadline tick is accepted; one tick later
is not. All timing uses integer ticks.

Menus, pause, perk picks, results and READY time never count as shift time. Gameplay sounds stop while paused.

## 12 Scoring, rounds and performance review

**Per delivery:** 100 + 25 if a ribbon was required + 25 if RUSH + time bonus + streak bonus, then multipliers.
- Time bonus: floor(20 × remaining / allowed), 0 to 20 (allowed = the order's assigned duration).
- Streak bonus: 5 × min(streak − 1, 4). An expiry resets the streak; rejections and recycling do not.
- Multipliers: JOLLY +10% (Christmas Spirit at 75% or more) and the Santa's Favour perk (+15% per stack).
- Optional extra ribbons earn nothing. Misses never subtract points.

**Rounds.** Quota 9 in round 1 (relaxed 6), +1 each round. Meet it and the next round starts after a perk pick, with
shorter deadlines and a harder bench; miss it and the run is over. Score carries across rounds. Best score and furthest
round are saved per mode.

**Christmas Spirit** (lasts the whole run): starts at 100. Delivery +4, coal delivery +4, missed order −20, botched toy
−8, +15 between rounds. At 0 the shift and the run end. Values in `tuning.json > spirit`.

**Perks.** After each passed round Santa offers 3 perk cards (4 if the quota was beaten by 2 or more); the player keeps
one for the run. Seven perks in `perks.json`, each stackable 2 or 3 times.

**Unlocks.** Lifetime totals (toys and coal delivered, furthest round, rounds cleared, runs played) unlock perks for
Santa to offer, hat colours, and a Head Start perk before round 1 (`unlocks.json`, saved in `progress.json`).

**Review** shows delivered, quota, missed, unfinished, score, best streak, round and mode. Labels by delivered count:
under 9 "Needs more cocoa", 9 to 11 "Shift survivor", 12 or more "Workshop legend". Santa's line never contradicts the
counts.

**Punching out.** At any time the elf can punch out at the punch clock (two presses). The shift ends there and is scored
exactly as if time had run out: live orders close as unfinished, letters not yet written never arrive, and the quota
decides the round. With the quota met, Santa signs off on it ("Clocking off early? Quota's met, so... fine.") and the
review notes the time left. There is no early-finish bonus yet; it trades a chance at more points for a safe pass
(**proposed:** a small bonus per 10 s saved, if playtests show nobody uses it).

**Relaxed mode:** same schedule, deadlines ×1.5, quota 6, a gentler bench, its own personal best. Never ranked or shamed.

**Tuning note:** in standard mode, with no deliveries every order expires before the shift ends; "unfinished" only
happens when orders are still live at 300 s.

## 15 Narrative and dialogue

Santa is cheerful in public and self-important at work; corporate language applied to Christmas problems. Mechanical
text stays plain and separate from flavour (`dialogue.json` holds both). Announcements fit two short lines. No real
companies, no required profanity.

| Cue | Santa line | Mechanical text |
|---|---|---|
| intro | Welcome aboard. We are one big festive family. Your break is next year. | Collect a train kit. |
| ribbon_warning | A memo from Branding. Apparently joy needs a bow. | In 5 seconds, new orders will require ribbons. |
| ribbon_start | Every present deserves presentation. Especially mine. | New orders require ribbons for 30 seconds. |
| rush_warning | I may have promised a very important child a very impossible deadline. | The next new order will be RUSH. |
| ribbon_second | Good news. We have standardized the festive compliance initiative. | New orders require ribbons for 30 seconds. |
| final_rush | One last tiny favor. It is only Christmas at stake. | The next new order will be RUSH. |
| quota_met | Excellent. I shall mention my leadership in the newsletter. | Quota reached. Keep going for a higher score. |
| review_pass | Another successful Christmas. My system works beautifully. | Shift complete. |
| review_miss | Let us call this a development opportunity. | Quota missed. Try another shift. |
| punched_out | Clocking off early? Quota's met, so... fine. I'll note your enthusiasm. | Punched out early. |
| naughty_order | (naughty-list order line) | Coal order. |
| spirit_out | (Christmas Spirit ran out) | The shift ends. |

Plus guide hints (`guide_*`), perk, unlock, spirit and ribbon messages. Each proposed event in Section 10.3 needs a
warning cue and a start cue in the same style. Quota-met fires once per round. Message priority: functional rejection,
policy change, flavour; messages never hide controls or stop movement.

## 16 Art direction

Cosy illustrated low-poly 3D: a warm log cabin at night lit by the fireplace, lanterns, windows and the tree. Every model
is generated by `tools/blender/make_models.py` and exported to `.glb` (rules in [blender-export.md](blender-export.md));
any missing model falls back to a grey-box shape.

- **Lighting:** up to 16 point lights from Blender (quality high/medium/low), tone mapping, fog, vignette, FXAA, glow
  halos (tree bulbs, the star) and window snow. Reduced motion stills the flicker, twinkle and snow.
- **Type:** one clear sans-serif for all UI text, loaded as an outline font with mipmaps so it stays sharp at any window
  size. Barlow SemiBold (OFL) ships with the game so Windows and macOS look the same; `Content/fonts/ui.ttf` overrides
  it, and system fonts are only a fallback if the file is missing. Key labels
  sit on their own high-contrast chips (gold on ink at the bench, ink on gold over the ribbon spools).
- **Palette** (UI and badges): snow #F4F0DC, ink #202934, pine #225E4C, berry #B94146, gold #E8BB57, wood #8C6043,
  ice #8EC9CF. Colour reinforces, never carries meaning alone: every requirement also has a shape, icon or label.
- **Characters:** an animated elf (idle, walk, work, hammer, carry) with selectable hat colours; Santa as a HUD portrait.
- **Toys:** train, bear and robot, each with three part models, an assembled form, a botched form and a ribbon bow.
- **Promotion:** a cover image and three gameplay screenshots or a GIF, captured from the finished game.

## 17 Audio direction and inventory

Brief, soft, toy-like effects. Delivery sounds rewarding; rejection informative, not punishing. Santa gets a distinctive
chime with a caption. No voice acting. No information is audio-only.

**Music (built):** "Cozy Christmas Game Ambience" (menus) and "Toy Factory Jingle" (shifts) by blueeyedmonkey, looping
with a crossfade. Used with the composer's permission; the condition is credit as "blueeyedmonkey" (Settings > Credits, README, game page).

**Mixer defaults:** master 0.8, effects 0.7, music 0.25. Repeats of the same effect are rate-limited (0.1 s).

## 18 Technical architecture

C# 14 on .NET 10, Raylib-cs 8.1 (zlib license), no engine, xUnit tests. MVC: views only read state, controllers turn
input into commands, and only `Core/Rules` changes the game.

```
src/SantaShift.Core/    Models, Rules (Simulation, OrderSystem, CraftingSystem, EventSystem, Scoring, TutorialGuide,
                        FloorPath, PlayerMotion), Content (JSON loading and validation), Persistence. No Raylib.
src/SantaShift.Game/    App (window, fixed-step loop, screens), Controllers, Views/World (3D, lighting, effects),
                        Views/UI (HUD, menus), Services (input, audio, assets), Content/data|models|audio
tests/SantaShift.Tests/ Rule, content and persistence tests (193 as of this version)
tools/blender/          Model generators and the export tool with rule checks
source-assets/blender/  .blend sources (not shipped)
docs/                   GDD, decision log, Blender export guide, playtests
```

A shipment changes run state once and emits a `SimEvent`; HUD, sound and particles respond without changing the score.
Static box collisions only, no physics engine. Everything is bundled locally: no internet, API key or login at runtime.

**Frame loop.** The rules step at a fixed 60 Hz; the game draws at the player's frame rate (120 by default). Frames drawn
between two steps place the elf and camera part of the way from the last step to the current one (`FrameClock`), so
motion stays smooth at any rate without changing the rules. `FramePacer` holds the cap to the frame (Raylib's own
limiter oversleeps to 118 or 119).

Commands: `dotnet build`, `dotnet run --project src/SantaShift.Game`, `dotnet test`, or double-click
`Play Santa Shift.cmd` (builds Release and starts the game). Release: `dotnet publish` (Section 27, **not set up**).
Debug flags: `--profile` (uncapped), `--profile-capped` (frame pacing at the set rate), `--guide`, `--perks`,
`--unlocks`, `--settings N` and others.

## 20 Performance and platform

- **Target:** a solid 120 fps (the default frame rate setting) on an ordinary laptop. Measured on the development PC
  (GTX 1660 Ti laptop, on mains power, 1280 × 720 window at 150% 3D resolution with FXAA): 737 fps uncapped (1.4 ms
  per frame); at the 120 cap, 120 fps with every frame within 9.1 ms. About 4.5 KB allocated per frame, no stutter
  from garbage collection.
- **Laptops on battery:** NVIDIA Battery Boost (and similar power savers) caps games at 30 fps on battery, whatever
  the game asks for. Plug in, or turn Battery Boost off in the NVIDIA app, to get 120.
- **Weak machines:** Settings > Game > 3D resolution, frame rate (60), lighting quality (`render.json`) and smooth edges.
- **60 Hz displays:** a 120 cap on a 60 Hz screen draws twice per refresh; the picture is smoother in motion but can
  tear. Settings > Game > Frame rate > Match display turns on VSync instead.
- **Platform:** Windows 10/11 x64. Self-contained build so no .NET install is needed. Handle resize, minimise, focus loss
  and a missing audio device gracefully. Internal size budget: compressed download under 100 MB.
- macOS and Linux builds are possible with Raylib but untested and not promised.

## 21 Accessibility and usability

- Every toy, requirement and policy uses text plus an icon or shape; colour is never the only signal (ribbon colours
  are also named on cards).
- Essential HUD text around 20 logical pixels or larger on the 960 × 540 UI canvas.
- Keyboard-only and controller-only play, full rebinding, visible menu focus.
- Immediate pause, separate music and effects volume, master mute, reduced motion, relaxed mode, camera follow options
  for trackpad users, adjustable look speed.
- No flashing, no forced camera shake, no tiny timers, no jokes over order information.
- The guide states one task at a time; failed actions explain how to recover without losing the item.
- Not screen-reader accessible; never claimed.

## 24 Test plan and playtest protocol

Automated tests protect rules whose failures lose progress, create unfair results or break restart (193 tests across
items, delivery, timing, events, orders, score, storage, rounds, perks, spirit and unlocks, supply and the shift sack,
ribbon colours, the bench, naughty and nice, the tutorial, the punch clock, settings and content loading). Drawing code
is not unit-tested; check it with screenshots and play.

**Scoring examples:** first ordinary delivery with 27.5 s left of 55 = 110. Second consecutive ribbon rush delivery with
22.5 s left of 45 = 165 (100 + 25 + 25 + 10 + 5).

**Manual release matrix:** fresh install on a second PC; no audio device; muted play; keyboard-only and controller-only
menus; window resize, minimise and fullscreen; focus loss; relaxed mode; save folder read-only; final-second shipment;
restart from every end state (quota missed, Spirit ran out, quit); the lesson both on and off. Test the actual zip, not
the dev build.

**Playtests:** start from the title without coaching; watch the first minute; let a full run finish. Record where they
hesitate, what they think Santa changed, whether rejected deliveries made sense, rounds reached, and whether they want
another go. Ask "What did the ribbon announcement change?" rather than explaining it.

**Targets:** first delivery within 90 s; every tester can name the carried item and the next action; 2 of 3 explain the
ribbon rule. Records go in `docs/playtests/` with build commit, date, tester experience, mode, result, problem, severity
and next change. Fix blockers, then ambiguity, then balance, then decoration.
